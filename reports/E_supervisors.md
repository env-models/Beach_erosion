Open-access author PDF: https://brgm.hal.science/hal-01719953 .

The paper's **"Eroded Volume Indicator (EVI)"** is the 2017 internship indicator: same site (Truc Vert), same forcing (1/16 ρ g Hs² Δt summed while the dynamic water level exceeds HAT), same two-model "pre-storm state" approach. BRGM report **RP-67418-FR** (Feb 2019) then **cites Gillet (2017) by name** as one of the two internship studies that informed the operational indicator. The operational indicator, however, **dropped the eroded-volume part** and kept a threshold matrix based on water level and wave power instead.

**Lineage verdict: builds on it.** The JCR 2018 paper builds on it directly (it is the EVI, co-authored by Raphaël). RP-67418-FR builds on it indirectly (it cites it as a design input, but the operational indicator does not use the volume regression).

Practical consequences for the project:
1. The new paper is a **follow-up to a published paper with Raphaël and both of Nicolae Lerma's teams on it**, not a revival of an unpublished student report. Cite Nicolae Lerma et al. (2018) as the reference for the EVI. The thesis is the detailed source.
2. Raphaël **already has a peer-reviewed proceedings paper** (JCR special issue). It belongs on his ORCID and portfolio now, independent of the new paper.
3. The JCR paper's own conclusion asks for "more extensive validation… for a wide range [of] single storms and storm clusters". The RP report (Annex 3) says it is "difficult to link the erosion-impact indicator to the erosion that actually occurred… because field observations bracketing storms are rare" and that clusters make it harder. **That is exactly the gap our project fills.**
4. **HAL metadata error:** HAL record hal-01719953 (and the PDF's cover page) gives DOI `10.2112/SI85-001.1`. Crossref resolves that DOI to a *different paper* (Woo et al., Nakdong River estuary). The correct DOI is **10.2112/SI85-134.1** (Crossref: JCR vol. 85, pp. 666–670, May 2018). Use the correct one everywhere.

### 1.1 TOP PRIORITY — the EWS paper and RP-67418-FR

#### (a) Nicolae Lerma et al. (2018), JCR SI 85 — "Towards the Development of a Storm Erosion EWS for the French Aquitaine Coast"

- **Venue:** International Coastal Symposium (ICS) 2018, Busan, Korea. Published in *Journal of Coastal Research* Special Issue 85, pp. 666–670 (it is not Coastal Dynamics, ICCE or JNGCGC). Received 30 Nov 2017, accepted 10 Feb 2018.
- **Authors and affiliations:** BRGM Orléans (Nicolae Lerma, Muller, Decarsin, **Gillet**), BRGM Pessac (Bulteau, Paris), EPOC/Univ. Bordeaux (Biausque, Sénéchal, Castelle).
- **Access:** full text downloaded from HAL (`ICS2018-FinalPaper-A1578-Nicolae-Lerma.pdf`, 6 pages). It was read in full.
- **Content (all quotes are original English):**
  - The EWS has two levels. Regional bulletins fire when 2 of 3 thresholds (η_st, Hs, Tp) are exceeded at one or more locations. At local scale it uses two indicators: the **CRI** (Collision Regime Indicator, adapted from Harley et al. 2016's Safe Corridor Width, which was Decarsin's 2016 work) and the **EVI**.
  - Forcing: MARC/MARS2DH tide + surge every 5 km along the 10 m isobath; WW3 waves on the 50 m isobath. "the wave energy density per length unit of wave crest and over a given period of time is estimated as: ΣE = ∫₀ᴺ (1/16) ρ g Hs² Δt … with ρ … 1030 kg/m³ … N: the considerate time in hours." → **The published EVI carries the same unit ambiguity flagged in HANDOFF §2.1 item 1** (1/16 factor, Δt in hours). The revisit should state this correction openly.
  - Runup: Stockdon et al. (2006), with the regime chosen by the Iribarren number. Slope is taken between η_st and the dune foot.
  - EVI definition: "The potential Eroded volume indicator (EVI) provides an estimation of the storm-driven eroded volume of sediment from the offshore wave energy density and the pre-storm beach profile volume inspired from Splinter et al. (2014). It considers a relationship between eroded volumes and the wave energy … released on the upper part of intertidal beach (when η_d exceeds the local HAT level)."
  - Data/split: "data collected at Truc Vert beach are analyzed during the period covering 2009-2013 (39 erosive storm events) and the performance of the EVI is evaluated on the 2013-2015 period (13 events), including the extreme storms of the winter 2013-2014."
  - Result: "the one that best reproduces eroded volumes (considerate along the entire cross shore profile), over the 2014-2015 periods is based on two linear models depending on the available sand prone to be eroded. The first model is applied if the pre-storm profile is already eroded, the second is applied for supplied pre-storm profile. The state of the profile is determined comparing the available pre-storm profile with the inter-seasonal typical mean profile. This mixed model presents interesting performances (**R² = 0.83; RMSE = 7.7 m³/m; NRMSE = 22%**) … A simpler approach without consideration of the pre-storm morphologic state of the profile, leads to less reliable results, with (**R² = 0.70; RMSE = 10.3 m³/m; NRMSE = 23%**)."
  - Limits stated by the authors: "it requires a pre-storm profile and a multi-annual and multi-seasonal survey long enough to establish a mean profile". Conclusion: "calibration and validation of the local indicators were limited and more extensive validation is needed … to consider a wider range of storms and storm clusters or to integrate more detailed hydro-morphodynamic modelling."
  - The paper does **not** cite the thesis itself (no "Gillet 2017" in its reference list), but Gillet is a co-author and the EVI section is the thesis method.
  - It cites two BRGM reports relevant to Agent B: **RP-63797-FR** (Bulteau et al. 2014, impact of the 2013–14 winter on the Aquitaine coast; public PDF confirmed at `infoterre.brgm.fr/rapports/RP-63797-FR.pdf`, HTTP 200, 23.7 MB) and **RP-63802-FR** (Bulteau et al. 2016, exposure to marine flooding) `[verify: not downloaded]`.

**Discrepancies with the thesis, to pass to Agent A (§2.1 item 9, R vs R²):**

| Quantity | Thesis (Gillet 2017) | JCR 2018 paper |
|---|---|---|
| Calibration events | 37 resolvable (51 storms), 2009–2013 | "39 erosive storm events", 2009–2013 |
| Validation events | 2014–2015 | "13 events", 2013–2015 |
| PPT (two-model) skill | "coefficient de corrélation … 0.85", RMSE 7.5 m³/m (thesis p. 39–40); abstract says "R²=0.85" | **R² = 0.83**, RMSE 7.7 m³/m, NRMSE 22% |
| Model without antecedent state | TPTE "0.61", RMSE 11.9 m³/m | **R² = 0.70**, RMSE 10.3 m³/m, NRMSE 23% |

The numbers are close but not identical, which suggests the model was re-run between the thesis (summer 2017) and the paper submission (Nov 2017). The paper explicitly says **R²**, while the thesis body says "coefficient de corrélation" (i.e. R). Agent A should treat the JCR numbers as the **published baseline** to reproduce and should report both.

- **Passages relevant to the lineage** (translated; French originals in the footnotes):
  - §3.2.3 (p. 43): "Based on the literature review of storm indicators … and on the synthesis of the work carried out during the first project (**project AP15AQI035, Decarsin, 2016, Gillet, 2017**), and given the context of the natural sand barriers of the Aquitaine coast, it was decided that a scale of erosive impacts on the beach–dune system, linked to the water level reaching the dune-foot elevation, would be the relevant choice of indicator for the Aquitaine sandy coast."[^1]
  - Reference list (pp. 71–72): "Gillet, R. (2017) – Elaboration d'un indicateur hydro-géomorphologique : un outil pour prévoir le risque d'érosion lors de tempêtes sur les plages sableuses aquitaines, Mémoire de stage de fin d'études, MASTER 2 … « DYNAMIQUE DES MILIEUX ET RISQUES », Université Paris Diderot, 63p." **The citation is confirmed.**
  - §3.2.4 (pp. 51–52): the operational indicator combines (i) **wave power**, "a proxy proportional to Hs²Tp, called the wave energy flux (CGE) transmitted per unit crest length", with thresholds of **100 and 200 kW/m**; (ii) the still water level against a "hydro" threshold (**PMVE**, spring high water at tidal coefficient 95); and (iii) the dynamic level (tide + surge + Stockdon setup/swash) against a "morpho" threshold, the **5th percentile of dune-foot elevation** per coastal section from the 2014/2016/2017 LiDAR. A non-symmetric matrix gives 4 levels (faible / moyen / fort / sévère).[^2]
  - Annex 3 (p. 105): "It should be noted that it is difficult to relate the erosion-impact indicator to the erosion that actually occurred during a storm, because field observations bracketing storms are rare. Likewise, successions of storms (clusters) make this harder, since field observations are generally made at the end of the cluster and not for each individual storm."[^3]
  - Annex 3, Table 6: sensitivity tests used either **Stockdon 2006** or a "**Nicolae-Lerma 2018**" runup formulation "(taking wave direction into account and modulation by the tide)". That item is *not* in the report's reference list and I could not find it on HAL or Crossref → `[verify]`. It may be an internal BRGM parameterisation derived from his SWASH work at Truc Vert (§1.2, item 8).
- **Does it use "volume érodé", "EVI", "énergie", "PHMA", "Truc Vert", "runup", "Stockdon"?** It uses PHMA/PMVE as reference levels, Stockdon 2006 for runup, energy flux (Hs²Tp, not Hs²) as forcing, and Truc Vert as a section (n°34, "LEGE CAP FERRET TRUC VERT"). The words **"volume érodé" and "EVI" do not appear**: the eroded-volume regression was not carried into the operational system.
