# N: Recent findings and overlap check, 2024 to October 2026

Agent N, session of 13–14 Oct 2026. This report looks for what Agents D and E may have missed. It asks three questions: (1) has anyone already done what we plan, (2) which methods published in 2024–2026 we should know about, and (3) which data have appeared since 2024. Everything here was found by searching OpenAlex, Crossref, HAL, theses.fr and the web (the queries are listed in §e).

**Verification rule.** `verified: yes` means the Crossref record (or DataCite, for datasets) matches the citation and the DOI resolves at doi.org with HTTP 302. All were checked on 13–14 Oct 2026 (procedure under "Reproduce" at the end). For grey literature without a DOI, `verified: yes (URL)` means the document was opened or downloaded from the URL given and its content was read. `no` / `[verify]` means the item is known only from a catalogue or a search snippet. Items already in `D_state_of_the_art.md` or `E_supervisors.md` are marked **(known)** and only appear where their ranking matters.

**Bottom line for question 1.** As of October 2026, **nobody has published an event-scale storm-erosion *volume* model for Truc Vert or Aquitaine, nor an evaluation of the OCNA "indice érosif" against observed volumes.** The **design ingredients are no longer novel**, though:
- event table + MLR with energy and antecedent state (Ibaceta & Harley 2024);
- XGBoost + SHAP on storm events (Thilakarathne et al. 2024);
- ML failing on small storm datasets (Thilakarathne et al. 2025);
- equilibrium features in ML (Siegelman et al. 2024);
- a two-stage classifier→regressor for volume (Kumar & Leonardi 2025);
- symbolic regression in coastal forecasting (Al Najar et al. 2026).

Closest to our site, Waeles et al. (2024) already did a winter-scale erosive-capacity reanalysis on the Gironde coast with Stockdon + Larson + XBeach.

## (d) What this changes for the paper (half page)

**Framing.** The paper should no longer present "energy + antecedent state + ML for per-storm erosion" as new. Since 2024 that design exists at Narrabeen (Ibaceta & Harley 2024, MLR on 276 storms) and Hasaki (Thilakarathne et al. 2024, XGBoost + SHAP on 347 storms). The strongest honest framing is threefold:
- **(i)** an audit and extended, leave-one-winter-out validation of a *published, operational-lineage* indicator (EVI 2018) and of the *live* OCNA "indice érosif" (still a threshold matrix in 2024, never validated against volumes);
- **(ii)** on a *macrotidal, rip-channelled, open* coast;
- **(iii)** with a *volume* target and calibrated uncertainty for warning classes.

The negative ML result is now **corroborated** by Thilakarathne et al. (2025): SVR/DNN R² ≈ 0.2 on 501 storms. Siegelman et al. (2024) gives the mechanism: ML beats equilibrium only with enough data. That turns "why ML doesn't win" into a supported, general message, not a local failure.

**Baselines to add.**
- **(1) An Ibaceta–Harley MLR** (cumulative storm energy + pre-storm volume/width + direction). It is cheap and mirrors the Narrabeen EWS model.
- **(2) A Larson-type wave-impact dune/upper-beach erosion model** driven by Stockdon runup. This is the physics baseline already used on the Gironde coast by Waeles et al. (2024).
- **(3) The OCNA indice érosif** (hours per class per event: PMVE / P5 % dune-foot × 100/200 kW/m), reported as a contingency table against observed ΔV classes.
- **(4) A convolution/memory-kernel form of the equilibrium model** (Vitousek et al. 2025), fitting a single decay time.

**Claims to soften.**
- "No SHAP/symbolic-regression study of storm erosion exists" becomes: SHAP on storm-erosion events exists (Thilakarathne 2024), and symbolic regression for shoreline forecasting exists (Al Najar et al. 2026). Only *SR for event-scale erosion volume* is still unclaimed.
- "Two-stage classifier→regressor is new" becomes: it has a macrotidal precedent for 6-monthly volumes (Kumar & Leonardi 2025). Ours is event-scale.
- "First reanalysis of erosive forcing in Aquitaine" should not be claimed (Waeles et al. 2024).
- Profile/strip-integrated ΔV may **underestimate local erosion on a 3D rip-channelled beach** (Harley et al. 2026). Say so, and quantify alongshore spread from the Castelle 2020 DEMs if possible.
- The equilibrium-model discussion must cite the Truc Vert line, Azorakos 2024–2026 (time-varying parameters).

**Timing.** At least four French PhDs started in 2024–2026 on hybrid or ML coastal-hazard forecasting (Marie/Castelle–Idier; Gardiol/Robin, PEPR IRIMA; Dif/Idier; Jean/Martins), and the Sénéchal group is publishing ANN-on-DYNALIT-profiles work (Mingorance et al. 2026). The niche will be contested within 1–2 years, so aim to submit by mid-2027. Raise this with Castelle and Nicolae Lerma, because one thesis is co-directed by Castelle.
